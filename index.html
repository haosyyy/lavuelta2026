💔 Pogačar crashed out of the race. Around 31km from Xeraco he brushed shoulders with another rider, lost the bars and went down hard on his shoulder and face. He tried to remount, could not go on, and walked to the ambulance. Hospital assessment found a concussion, a displaced left clavicle fracture, a stable C7 fracture of the neck and multiple abrasions. He abandoned with a lead of 3:50 — the first Grand Tour he has ever failed to finish.

In a bunch sprint on a day full of crashes, Bryan Coquard edged Mads Pedersen for the first Grand Tour stage win of his career, at 34. Enric Mas inherited the red jersey — the first Spaniard to lead the Vuelta since Jesús Herrada in 2018, and the first time Mas has led a Grand Tour.

Where the race could be won

Five days that are built to shape the general classification.

Stage 3

Font Romeu

The first summit finish of the race, reached after a first category climb over the Col de Mont-Louis. Too early to win the Vuelta, but the first gaps between GC riders can open here.

1,800m alt. · 166.7 km
Stage 9

Alto de Aitana

Six climbs across 187.5 km before a category-1 summit finish above the Costa Blanca. Route designer Fernando Escartín has warned that "the heat could make this already challenging stage even tougher."

6 categorised climbs
Stage 12

Calar Alto

The highest finish of the 2026 route, over 2,100m above sea level in the Sierra de los Filabres, reached straight off the back of the Alto de Velefique.

2,100m+ alt.
Stage 18

Jerez de la Frontera

A 32.5km individual time trial along the Bay of Cádiz in the final week, when the GC is usually settling — coastal wind could still turn the standings upside down.

32.5 km · Individual TT
Stage 20

Collado del Alguacil

The only climb in the race rated Categoría Especial, on the penultimate day. A brutal, near-double-digit-gradient finish built to decide the race with almost nothing left to give.

Categoría Especial · 8.3km at 9.8%

Contenders

Form and headlines heading into the Grand Départ — written as of 21 August 2026 and kept as a snapshot of the pre-race picture, not updated stage by stage. Jonas Vingegaard, the 2025 champion, is not defending his title after breaking his collarbone at the Tour de France.

Tadej Pogačar

UAE Team Emirates-XRG
Chasing the Grand Slam

Fresh off a record-tying fifth Tour de France title, Pogačar returns to the Vuelta for the first time since his 2019 debut, when he finished third and took the young rider jersey. A win in Granada would complete his set of all three Grand Tour titles.

Primož Roglič

Red Bull–BORA–hansgrohe
Riding for a record

A four-time Vuelta champion (2019, 2020, 2021, 2024), Roglič skipped this year's Tour de France to build toward a fifth title — which would make him the race's outright record holder, past Roberto Heras. At 36, and with a disrupted preparation, his form is something to watch rather than assume: his last win came at last year's Volta a Catalunya.

Enric Mas

Movistar Team
Spain's nearly man, at home

Mas has finished runner-up at the Vuelta three times without ever taking the top step, and leads a Movistar team built around him on home roads. A difficult Giro d'Italia this year complicates the picture, but a fifth place at the Vuelta a Burgos suggests his form is coming back at the right time.
